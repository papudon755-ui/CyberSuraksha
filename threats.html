Threat library

Know your enemy

Most online frauds reuse the same few tricks. Learn what each one looks like, which red flags give it away, and exactly what to do.

1. Phishing emails & messages

What it is

Fake emails, SMS or WhatsApp messages that pretend to be your bank, a delivery company or a government office, asking you to click a link or share details.

Red flags

  • Urgent threats: "account blocked", "last warning", "act in 2 hours"
  • Links that are shortened or look slightly misspelled
  • Generic greeting like "Dear customer" instead of your name
  • Attachments you did not ask for

What to do

Do not click. Open your bank's official app yourself or call the official helpline to check.

2. OTP fraud

What it is

A caller pretends to be bank staff, a delivery agent or tech support and tricks you into reading out the OTP sent to your phone.

Red flags

  • Anyone asking for your OTP — for any reason
  • Pressure: "share it now or your account will be closed"
  • Calls from unknown mobile numbers claiming to be the bank

What to do

Hang up and never share the OTP. Real bank staff never ask for it. Call your bank's official number if worried.

3. UPI & QR code scams

What it is

A "buyer" or "prize" sender asks you to scan a QR code or approve a collect request to receive money — but it actually sends money from your account.

Red flags

  • Being asked to enter your UPI PIN to receive money
  • QR codes sent by strangers for "refunds" or "prizes"
  • Collect requests appearing in your UPI app unexpectedly

What to do

Never enter your PIN to receive money. Decline unknown collect requests and verify the person through a separate call.

4. Ransomware

What it is

Harmful software, often from a bad download or attachment, that locks your files or screen and demands payment to unlock them.

Red flags

  • Unexpected attachments, especially .exe or .zip files
  • "Free" software or movies from unknown websites
  • Pop-ups claiming your device is infected — click to fix

What to do

Do not pay. Disconnect from the internet, and take the device to a trusted technician. Keep regular backups of important files.

5. Fake customer-care numbers

What it is

Scammers place fake helpline numbers on search results and social media. When you call, they ask for account details, OTPs or a "verification" payment.

Red flags

  • Numbers found in random search results or comments, not the official site
  • Being asked to download a screen-sharing app
  • Requests for OTP, PIN or advance payment to "resolve" your issue

What to do

Only use numbers from the company's official website or app. Never install apps at a stranger's request.

🧮

6. Social engineering

What it is

Instead of hacking computers, the scammer hacks you — using trust, fear, greed or sympathy to make you hand over money or information yourself.

Red flags

  • Too-good-to-be-true job offers, prizes or investment returns
  • Emotional pressure: fear, urgency, secrecy ("don't tell anyone")
  • Someone you just met online asking for money or gift cards

What to do

Slow down. Talk to a family member before acting, and never send money to someone you have not met in person.
